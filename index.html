Getting started

3D GPX Viewer shows a 3D model of your route inside a clear resin dome, like a paperweight, right in your web browser.

There's nothing to install, and nothing you open is uploaded anywhere.

  1. Open the website. A sample route loads straight away.
  2. Open the Examples tab and tap an example to see it, or go to the Model tab and choose Upload a .glb or .3mf to use your own model.
  3. Drag to turn it around, then try the Scene and Light tabs to change how it looks.

It works on computers, tablets and phones in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox.

Examples

Tap any example card to open it, set up exactly as intended.

Each example loads with its own dome, background, lighting and camera angle already in place. After opening an example you can still change anything in the other tabs; tap the card again to go back to how it was set up.

No examples yet?

If the tab says there are no examples yet, none have been published. You can still upload your own model in the Model tab.

Model

Load your own model and shape the dome around it.

Loading a model

Tap Upload a .glb or .3mf and pick a file, or drag the file onto the page. The viewer accepts .glb files (from 3D apps and most online model makers) and .3mf files (from 3D-printing slicers such as Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer). A dome is added over the model automatically.

Shaping the dome

SettingWhat it does
Resin outlineFollow the model traces the model's outline; Circle makes a round dome.
Top edgeDome curves smoothly, Chamfer gives a crisp angled edge, Flat gives a sharp square edge.
HeightHow far the resin rises above the model.
Dome size or Chamfer sizeHow big the curve or angled edge is. The resin grows taller if needed so it always covers the model.
ReflectionsHow strongly the resin reflects light.
Bend the background through the resinMakes the background warp through the resin like real glass. Turn it off for the sharpest view of the model.
RefractionHow strong the bending is. Shown when bending is on.

Other switches

Tabletop and shadowShows the surface the model sits on. Turn it off to make the model float.
Show domeHides or shows the resin so you can compare.
Auto-rotateTurns the model slowly by itself.

Saving your model

Tap Download with dome to save a .glb file of your model with the dome built in, ready to open in other 3D apps.

Scene

Choose what the paperweight sits on and what's behind it.

Tip

Patterned backgrounds show off the clear resin best, because you can see the lines bend through the dome.

Pattern size and wall

Pattern size appears for patterns and abstract images. Slide it left for a finer pattern, which gives a stronger bending effect through the dome.

Wall close behind the paperweight stands a wall just behind the model, whichever way you turn it, so there's always something close for the resin to bend. Wall distance moves it nearer or further away.

Using your own photo

Tap Upload a photo, or drag an image onto the page, then choose how to use it:

BackdropThe photo fills the screen behind the model, like a photo of your desk. Turn and zoom until the model sits naturally on the surface.
TabletopThe photo covers the table and the wall, repeated like a pattern.
WallThe photo appears once on the wall behind the model, like a poster.
360°For panorama photos, which wrap all the way around.

Use camera shows your device's live camera view behind the model. Your browser asks for permission first.

AR: see it on your desk

Place the paperweight on a real table through your phone's camera, at real size.

First choose how big it should be: 8 cm, 12 cm or 20 cm across.

On an Android phone (Chrome)

  1. Tap Place on a surface (AR) and allow camera access.
  2. Move the phone slowly over a table until white rings appear on it.
  3. Tap to put the paperweight there, then walk around it.
  4. Tap Move it to choose a new spot, or Exit AR to go back.

On an iPhone or iPad (Safari)

  1. Tap View in AR. Apple's AR viewer opens.
  2. Tap AR at the top, point at a table, and the paperweight appears on it.
About AR

In AR the resin shows reflections but doesn't bend the real world behind it; phones don't let web pages do that. If neither button appears, your browser doesn't support AR. Try Chrome on Android or Safari on iPhone.

Tips and troubleshooting

Quick fixes for the most common questions.

ProblemTry this
The model looks blurry through the domeTurn off Bend the background through the resin in the Model tab for the sharpest view.
I can't see the resinPick a patterned or dark background in the Scene tab, and turn bending on.
My model won't loadMake sure it's a .glb or .3mf file. If it mentions Draco or KTX2 compression, export it again without compression. Very large models may be too big.
My 3MF colours are missingColours painted with a slicer's paint tool aren't shown; each object shows its filament colour instead.
The page looks out of dateRefresh the page, or open it in a private window.
The camera or AR won't startAllow camera access when your browser asks, and check the site address starts with https.
Tip

For the best-looking view, try a pattern or a table surface from the Scene tab with the Window or Golden hour lighting.

About admin

Admins choose the examples visitors see in the Examples tab. How you sign in and publish depends on where the viewer runs.

Where the viewer runsSigning inWhen visitors see changes
Your own websiteWith the admin password, at the bottom of the Examples tab.After you export the examples and upload them to the website.
Inside ClaudeAutomatic for anyone who can edit the page.Straight away.

Most of this guide covers your own website. Differences for the Claude-hosted page are noted where they apply.

Signing in

Open the Examples tab, enter the admin password at the bottom and tap Sign in.

Once you're signed in, the admin tools appear in place of the password box, and each example card gets buttons to reorder and delete it. Tap Admin guide there to open this guide in the app, and Sign out when you're finished.

You stay signed in until you sign out or close the browser tab. On the Claude-hosted page there's no password: if you can edit the page, the admin tools are already there.

Adding examples

An example saves the model together with how it looks, so visitors see it exactly as you set it up.

  1. In the Model tab, tap Upload a .glb or .3mf and choose the model.
  2. Set it up how you want visitors to see it: dome shape and height in the Model tab, background in the Scene tab, lighting in the Light tab.
  3. Turn and zoom the model to the camera angle you want. The example card uses this view as its picture.
  4. In the Examples tab, type a name and tap Add current view as an example.
Saved with the exampleNot saved
The model, dome, background, lighting, reflections, refraction, AR size and camera angleYour own uploaded photos and the live camera background. Choose a built-in background for examples.
Model size

On the Claude-hosted page, models used as examples can be up to about 15 MB. On your website the limit is GitHub's 25 MB per file when uploading through the browser.

Ordering and deleting

Examples appear in the order you set, left to right and top to bottom.

Move earlier or laterTap ↑ or ↓ on the card.
DeleteTap ✕ on the card, then tap it again within five seconds to confirm.
Change an exampleOpen it, make your changes, add it again with the same name, then delete the old one.

Publishing to your website

On your website, examples you add are saved in your browser as a draft. Visitors only see them after you publish.

  1. In the Examples tab, tap Export examples for your website. This downloads elevate-examples.zip.
  2. Unzip it. You get a folder called examples, containing examples.json and a models folder.
  3. In the GitHub repository, choose Add file › Upload files, drag in the whole examples folder (not the zip) and commit.
  4. Wait for the green tick in the Actions tab.

Repeat these steps whenever you add, remove or reorder examples. The new examples folder replaces the old one.

Checking it worked

Open the site in a private window, or tap Sign out: while signed in you see your drafts, not the published examples. You can also open your-site-address/examples/examples.json; if it shows text starting with {"examples":, they're live.

Drafts stay in one browser

Drafts are kept only in the browser you made them in. Clearing that browser's data, or switching to another computer, loses unpublished drafts, so export after each session. Signing in on a new device starts from the published examples.

Updating the website

When there's a new version of the viewer, replace the files in your GitHub repository.

  1. Unzip the new 3d-gpx-viewer.zip on your computer.
  2. In the repository, choose Add file › Upload files, drag in everything from the unzipped folder and commit. Upload the files, never the zip itself: GitHub doesn't unpack zips.
  3. Wait for the green tick in the Actions tab, then check the site in a private window.

Your published examples folder isn't part of the download, so it stays as it is. If you changed the admin password, set it again in the new index.html before committing (see the next section).

Releases

To keep a record of each version, attach the zip to a GitHub release (Releases › Draft a new release). Releases don't change the live site; only the files in the repository do.

Changing the admin password

The default password is elevate-admin. Change it before you share the site.

Only a coded version (hash) of the password is stored, in index.html on the line starting window.ELEVATE_ADMIN_HASH, so the password itself never appears in the code.

  1. Make the hash of your new password. On a Mac, open Terminal and run printf '%s' 'new-password' | shasum -a 256. On Windows, use the PowerShell command in the README.
  2. Copy the long string of letters and numbers it prints (leave out the trailing -).
  3. In the repository, open index.html, tap the pencil icon, replace the old value between the quotes, and commit.
About security

A website without a server can't truly lock anything, so the password only hides the admin tools. Nothing reaches visitors until someone with access to your GitHub repository uploads files, so your repository login is what really protects the site. Use a long password, as a short one could be guessed from its hash.
